# Kernel export to the MLspike team — record + open concerns (2026-07-20)

What was handed to the MATLAB/MLspike team on 2026-07-20, and every concern raised in
producing it. Written because the concerns matter more than the delivery: the export is
easy to regenerate, but the reasons to distrust parts of it were discovered once and would
otherwise live only in a chat log.

Answers `team_colonel_kernel/REQUEST_2026-07-20_matlab_kernels.md`. Companion to
[`kernel-review-baseline-2026-07-19.md`](kernel-review-baseline-2026-07-19.md), which is the
human verdict this rests on.

## What was delivered

`<bus>/kernels/colonel_kernels_v1.{csv,json}` + `README.md`, generated by
[`scripts/dataset-summary/export_kernels.mjs`](../../scripts/dataset-summary/export_kernels.mjs).

The 8 human-identified baseline kernels, **ROI 1**, one row each: the requested
`slice_id, a, tau, ton, sat, fit_ok` schema plus 23 more columns, and full waveforms
(`fv`, `pm`, `shaped`, `sta`) with a lag axis and per-region `dt`.

**ROI 1 is settled.** Tony confirmed directly (2026-07-20) that these are human-identified
**ROI 1** kernels — that is the column reviewed and the column the kernel was identified in.
Per ADR-0011/0018 that closes the question *for these eight*. The open ROI-1 item in the
2026-07-19 review is therefore resolved-by-author here; what remains open is not the labelling
but the screen's disagreement with it (below).

## The findings that should outlive this session

### 1. The parametric fit fails on 7 of 8 — the headline concern

`pm_fit_quality` is `good` for **one** recording (`20250925_233`). Five `failed`
(`80`, `209`, `235`, `237`, `272`), two `poor` (`151`, `181`).

| failure mode | recordings |
|---|---|
| R² ≤ 0 (no better than predicting the trace mean) | `80`, `209`, `235`, `272` |
| `tau_rise` railed at its 1.0 s bound | `209`, `235` |
| did not converge | `237` |
| converged but R² < 0.5 | `151`, `181` |

**This is a real result about our Method 2 on dense-firing real data, not a scripting
artifact**, and it is the reason the delivery leads with "do not adopt `a`/`tau`." It
deserves a proper look: a double exponential is the standard forward model in this
literature, and ours fits one of eight recordings that a human says visibly contain kernels.
Either the model is too rigid for these traces, the baseline tilt defeats the least-squares
objective, or the fit needs a better initialization. **Unresolved — worth its own
investigation.**

### 2. Two recordings are physically implausible: `20260130_272`, `20250807_181`

Both have `fv_acausal_ratio > 1` (2.14 and 2.36) — more recovered *energy* at negative lags
than at positive ones. No real calcium kernel can look like that. They are on the human list
and were delivered with an `ACAUSAL` flag, but **the human verdict and this diagnostic
disagree on these two**, and that disagreement has not been adjudicated. `272` also has only
192 spikes, the fewest of the eight.

### 3. STA and deconvolution amplitudes disagree in both directions

Against `a_robust`, STA runs higher on 3 of the 7 recordings that have one and lower on 4:

| | `80` | `209` | `233` | `235` | `272` | `151` | `181` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| STA ÷ `a_robust` | 3.63× | 7.63× | 10.52× | 0.91× | 0.43× | 0.56× | 0.57× |

Because it is bidirectional it is **not** a calibration offset anyone can correct for. Two
methods that should agree on a per-spike amplitude do not, and we do not know why. This is
arguably a deeper problem than finding 1 and is currently undiagnosed.

### 4. `a_robust` is regularization-dependent and its stability was not checked

Every `fv_*` column comes from a Tikhonov solve at `LAMBDA_DEFAULT = 0.002`, and the export
runs with `stability: false`. ADR-0004 requires λ stay visible precisely because the
free-vector amplitude is a function of it. λ is now recorded in the export
(`fv_lambda`, `fv_lambda_stability_checked = no`), **but the sweep has not been run for these
eight**. A spot check during review found single-digit-% drift across λ ∈ [0.002, 3.0] on four
of them, which is reassuring but is not the same as having run it.

### 5. `20250926_237` has no STA — and that is mechanically explained

All 2213 eligible events are rejected by the STA's 1.0 s overlap guard at ~1.8 Hz mean rate.
Not a bug. It independently corroborates the MLspike team's claim that 237 fires too densely
to isolate unitary events, and it is why the deconvolved kernel is the only handle there.
237 is also the cleanest of the eight by acausal ratio (0.008).

### 6. Doc gap: no ADR covers the absence of a saturation term

The delivery states our forward model has no saturation term. Nothing in the ADR set says so.
ADR-0006 decides linear-**vs-circular** convolution — a different sense of "linear" — and
grepping all 37 ADRs for "saturation" returns nothing; the only mention is `FOUNDATIONS.md`
scoping it to the ADR-0021 parametric foil. A near-miss: an early draft cited ADR-0006 for
this and would have shipped a misattribution to another team. **Either an ADR should record
the linear-response assumption, or FOUNDATIONS should state it project-wide.**

### 7. Our screen disagrees with the human on 7 of 8 — now a screen problem

With ROI 1 settled, the screen's disagreement is a finding about the screen. It calls ROI 1
plausible only for `20241004_80`; `20250807_181` has decent kernels only in non-ROI-1 columns;
the other six have **no** decent kernel in any column. And for `20250904_209` and
`20250926_235` the screen never examined baseline at all — it scores the region with the most
targeted-cell APs, which for those two was senktide. Consistent with the "baseline tilt hides
kernels" hypothesis from when the screen was built. Still owed: the baseline-restricted re-run
listed in the 2026-07-19 review.

## Murderboard review report (per ADR-0037)

The two handoff documents plus the new analysis code went through
[`docs/doc_review_process.md`](../doc_review_process.md) — full team, since the deliverable
rests on new code: claim/data verifier, consistency auditor, adversarial reviewer + line
editor, methods/domain expert, reuse auditor.

**Dimensions checked:** every quantitative claim against the regenerated CSV/JSON; ADR
citations; internal consistency across README ⇄ status log ⇄ JSON; method invariants in the
recovery path; reuse against `slice_lib.mjs`/`readout.js`.

**Issues found and fixed — 14.** The ones that mattered:

- **A wrong statistic shipped in two places.** "STA disagrees by up to ~16×
  (`20250904_209`: 0.082 vs free-vector 0.011)" — that ratio is 7.6×. The 16× came from
  comparing STA against the *parametric* `a`, not the free-vector value the sentence named.
  Replaced with the full bidirectional distribution (finding 3).
- **Fabricated bound + wrong epsilon in the fit-quality gate.** The script hand-rolled
  `BOUNDS` including a `tauDecay` minimum of `0.02` that **does not exist** — `PARAM_BOUNDS`
  has no `tauDecayMin` (the floor is the dynamic `tauRise + gapMin`) — and used an absolute
  `1e-6` tolerance where canonical `tauRailed` (ADR-0025) uses a relative 1%. Now imports and
  calls `tauRailed`. Classifications are unchanged on all 8 rows, so nothing shipped wrong,
  but the gate the recipients are told to trust was re-derived rather than reused.
- **Miscount:** "six of these fail" was neither the `failed` count (5) nor the non-`good`
  count (7). Now stated as 1 good / 5 failed / 2 poor.
- **The `poor` category was never documented** — two of the eight fell into a bucket with no
  stated meaning or usage guidance.
- **Screen claim overstated:** "the screen places the kernel in a non-ROI-1 column for 7 of 8"
  was wrong; six of those seven have the screen finding *nothing anywhere*. Corrected.
- **Three artifacts contradicted each other on ROI 1** — README said settled, the status entry
  asked the other team to act, and the JSON `caveats` (which travels with the data) said open.
  All three now say the same thing.
- **Undefined quantities defined:** R², acausal ratio (an *energy* ratio, was described as
  "mass"), `a_robust`'s baseline correction, and the fact that fv/pm peak lags are
  causal-only by construction while `sta_peak_lag_s` can be negative (it is −1.9 s on `151`).
- **16 of 26 CSV columns were undocumented** — full glossary added; `win_*` renamed
  `region_*`; `sta_empty` added; `pm_theta.a` renamed `scale_coeff` (it is the scale term, not
  the peak — differs from CSV `a` by >100× on `237`); empty STA now emits an empty `times`
  vector so the arrays always zip; `dt` now taken from the region rather than the recording.
- **Overreach softened:** "far more consistent" → "somewhat", with the actual spread and CV.
- **Method 3 warning added** — ADR-0021 flags `shaped` as needing the most scrutiny, and we
  were shipping it with no caveat.

**Residual ⚠ flags:**

1. **The λ-stability sweep has not been run** for these eight (finding 4). Flagged in the
   delivery as `fv_lambda_stability_checked = no`.
2. **Findings 1, 2 and 3 are unexplained**, not just undocumented. The delivery discloses them;
   it does not resolve them.
3. **No ADR records the no-saturation assumption** (finding 6).
4. The MLspike team's own prior global `a = 0.007, tau = 1.5 s` (2026-07-13) is quoted from
   their request; its derivation was not re-verified here.

## Data hygiene

Recording IDs, derived scalars and derived kernel waveforms only — no raw traces, no
per-sample trace dumps. The delivered files live on the Dropbox bus and are **not** committed
to this repo. Consistent with ADR-0022 and the `kernel-review-baseline` precedent.
